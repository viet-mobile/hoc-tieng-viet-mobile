A: ⟦Em khoẻ không?⟧ — Are you well? (to a younger person)
B: ⟦Vâng. Em khoẻ.⟧ — Yes. I'm well.
The relationship decides not only how you address the other person but also how you refer to yourself. The older A calls the younger B ⟦em⟧ (younger sibling), and B also calls themself ⟦em⟧ when speaking to A. Older speakers (⟦anh⟧, ⟦chị⟧) likewise refer to themselves as ⟦anh⟧, ⟦chị⟧ when talking with a younger person (⟦em⟧).
A: ⟦Chị khoẻ không?⟧ — Are you well, sister?
B: ⟦Ừ. Chị khoẻ.⟧ — Yeah. I'm well.
Statements follow the order "subject + verb / adjective". Unlike English ("subject + be + adjective"), the adjective comes straight after the subject to form a sentence. Really easy.
⟦Em đi.⟧ — I'm going. (said by the younger person)
⟦Anh khoẻ.⟧ — I'm (your older brother is) well.
Word: ⟦đi⟧ to go
Questions follow the order "subject + (có) + verb / adjective + không?" ("Is/does the subject …?"). ⟦có⟧ means "to have, there is", but in questions with ⟦không⟧ it comes before the verb or adjective as a question-forming element and can be left out.
⟦Chị có tiền không?⟧ — Do you have money, sister? (có = to have)
⟦Em (có) đi không?⟧ — Are you going? (có forms the question)
⟦Anh (có) khoẻ không?⟧ — Are you well, brother? (có forms the question)
Words: ⟦có⟧ to have, there is; question element | ⟦tiền⟧ money
